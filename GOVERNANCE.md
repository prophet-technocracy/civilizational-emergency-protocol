# CEP Governance — Founding Proposal

**Status:** Proposed model for discussion; not yet ratified.
**Intended direction:** Community-governed.

## 1. Authority and legitimacy

CEP's long-term direction should be governed by a pluralistic community under rules that are public, accessible and amendable. No founder, maintainer, donor, developer, AI agent or faction has inherent authority to speak for all participants or for a wider population.

The current GitHub repository is technically administered by its owner. This is a practical bootstrap arrangement, not a community mandate. Platform administration and community governance are distinct: repository permissions control infrastructure, while legitimate project decisions require transparent procedures.

## 2. Proposed governance layers

These are design proposals, not currently constituted bodies:

- **Open community:** anyone may read, discuss, propose, review and fork the work, subject to safety and conduct rules.
- **Working groups:** voluntary, time-bounded groups for research, engineering, safety, accessibility, documentation and mission work. Their scope and membership should be public where safe.
- **Maintainers:** trusted stewards responsible for repository hygiene, technical review and release operations. Maintainers do not gain authority over public policy merely through code permissions.
- **Community decision process:** a published process for deciding project-wide priorities, charter amendments and steward appointments.
- **Independent review:** external review for security, privacy, human-rights and other high-impact questions.

## 3. Decisions

Every significant proposal should include:
- the problem and intended beneficiaries;
- alternatives considered, including doing nothing;
- evidence, assumptions and uncertainties;
- impact on rights, privacy, safety and accessibility;
- conflicts of interest and funding disclosures where safe;
- implementation, evaluation and rollback plans.

The community should have time to deliberate before a decision. The final process—consensus-seeking, voting or a combination—must be specified and ratified before any vote is represented as binding.

## 4. Safeguards against capture

The governance design should address:
- concentration of administrative or financial power;
- sybil attacks and manipulation of online participation;
- donor, state, party or corporate capture;
- exclusion by language, disability, geography or connectivity;
- retaliation against dissenters;
- conflicts of interest and undisclosed delegation;
- AI-generated volume overwhelming human deliberation.

No token purchase, donation, contribution count, follower count or computational resource should buy extra civic authority. One account should not be treated as proof of one person or one legitimate vote without an appropriate, privacy-preserving process.

## 5. Moderation and appeals

Moderation should be rule-based, proportionate, documented and appealable. Criticism, peaceful disagreement and unpopular viewpoints are not misconduct. Threats, harassment, doxxing, spam and unlawful content may be moderated under published rules. Moderators should disclose relevant conflicts and recuse themselves where appropriate.

## 6. AI agents

AI agents may research, draft, test, translate and submit pull requests. They must identify themselves as automated where relevant, cite sources, disclose uncertainty and avoid fabricating evidence or identities. Agents do not receive independent votes, administrative privileges or authority to represent people. Human maintainers remain accountable for accepting changes.

## 7. Stewardship and technical permissions

Use least-privilege GitHub roles. Outside contributors should normally work through forks and pull requests. Protect the default branch, require review and automated checks once those checks exist, and keep recovery access secure. Administrative access should be limited, documented and periodically reviewed.

## 8. Ratification path

1. Invite public comments on this draft.
2. Publish a revision log and response to major objections.
3. Draft precise decision and membership rules.
4. Test the process on low-risk project decisions.
5. Obtain independent review of capture, privacy and accessibility risks.
6. Conduct a clearly scoped ratification process with published eligibility, quorum, method and appeal rules.
7. Publish the outcome, participation limitations and dissenting views.

Until this process is completed, this document remains a proposal. Repository merges are not ratification.
