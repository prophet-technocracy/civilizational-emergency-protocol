# CEP Vision

**Status:** Working draft for community discussion.

## The challenge

Societies facing rapid technological, institutional, environmental or political change often lack trustworthy ways to share evidence, deliberate across disagreement, coordinate useful work and hold decision-makers accountable. Information is fragmented, participation can be inaccessible, and concentrated power can outpace public oversight.

## The vision

CEP explores an open, modular civic coordination protocol that helps people:
1. understand shared challenges through accessible, sourced public knowledge;
2. deliberate across differences without coercion;
3. organise voluntary, pluralistic initiatives;
4. propose and compare institutional choices transparently;
5. coordinate practical public-interest projects;
6. track commitments, budgets and outcomes; and
7. hold systems and stewards accountable.

## What CEP is—and is not

CEP is intended as civic infrastructure and a community-governed open project. It is not a state, government, political party, election commission, intelligence service, substitute for independent journalism or civil society, or an authority entitled to speak for a population.

Software can support democratic processes; it cannot manufacture legitimacy. A platform poll is not automatically an election, a contributor vote is not a national mandate, and a popular proposal is not a substitute for rights and due process.

## Design commitments

- Open standards and auditable code where feasible.
- Human-centred, multilingual and accessible design.
- Privacy by design and minimal collection of personal data.
- Interoperability and the ability to leave or fork.
- Transparent governance, moderation and funding.
- Independent review for high-impact features.
- Clear separation between research, advocacy, service delivery and public authority.

## First implementation

Iran has been discussed as a possible first detailed country mission because of the project's prior focus. Any such work must remain a proposal, include diverse Iranian perspectives, protect people from foreseeable risks and leave all constitutional outcomes open to a free and credible public decision. CEP's global principles must not be defined by one country's political preferences.
