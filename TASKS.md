# CEP Task Board

**Status:** Initial agent task backlog; open for review.  
**How to use:** Read this file, then open the linked task brief before starting. Tasks are proposals for research and development—not mandates, endorsements or claims of public legitimacy.

## Start here

1. Read [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).
2. Read [Research Standards](agents/RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md).
3. Read [Founding Charter](FOUNDING-CHARTER.md) and [Governance](GOVERNANCE.md).
4. Select a task below and check the linked issue/PR activity for duplicate work.
5. State your intended approach in an issue or discussion before doing substantial work.
6. Work on a separate branch and submit a focused pull request.

## Task status definitions

- **Proposed:** suggested work; scope or priority may change.
- **Ready:** scope and deliverable are clear enough to begin.
- **In progress:** an agent or contributor has publicly taken responsibility.
- **Review:** a pull request is open for review.
- **Blocked:** a dependency, missing source or safety question prevents progress.
- **Complete:** the deliverable has been reviewed and merged. This does not mean its conclusions are universally accepted or community-ratified.

## General acceptance checklist

A task is not complete until its deliverable:
- answers the stated research question or explains why it cannot yet be answered;
- cites sources that can be checked;
- separates facts, author statements, interpretations, hypotheses and proposals;
- includes counterarguments, uncertainty and limitations;
- addresses rights, privacy, safety, accessibility and misuse;
- identifies unresolved questions and next steps;
- has been reviewed by at least one human contributor.

## Coordination rules

- One task may have several subtasks, but each subtask should have a distinct deliverable and owner.
- Agents should work independently where useful, then cross-review one another.
- Do not treat the number of agents, comments, citations or generated pages as evidence of truth or public support.
- Do not merge directly to `main`; use pull requests and human review.
- If new evidence changes a task's assumptions, update the brief openly rather than silently changing its purpose.
- Keep disagreements visible in the record. A synthesis should map agreement and disagreement, not manufacture consensus.
