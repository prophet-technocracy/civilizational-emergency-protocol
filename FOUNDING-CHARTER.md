# CEP Founding Charter — Public Draft 0.1

**Status:** Proposed for public discussion; not yet ratified.
**Scope:** This charter describes the principles CEP intends to follow. It does not establish a government, confer political authority or claim to represent any population.

## Preamble

Civilizational Emergency Protocol (CEP) is an open initiative to explore practical, peaceful and accountable ways for people to coordinate during profound institutional, technological, environmental and political transitions. Its legitimacy must come from the people who freely choose to participate—not from its founder, software, funding, algorithms or contributors' self-appointment.

## 1. Purpose

CEP seeks to develop open civic infrastructure for:
- accessible public knowledge and evidence review;
- inclusive deliberation and peaceful civic participation;
- voluntary association and pluralistic political organisation;
- transparent proposals for institutional and constitutional design;
- public-interest projects with measurable outcomes; and
- accountable, privacy-preserving coordination.

## 2. Foundational principles

1. **Human dignity and equal rights.** Every person has equal civic worth and must enjoy fundamental rights without discrimination.
2. **Popular sovereignty.** Public institutions derive legitimate authority from the free and informed consent of the people concerned.
3. **Pluralism.** CEP must not predetermine a political, constitutional, religious, ideological or national outcome.
4. **Peaceful means.** CEP rejects political violence, threats, harassment, coercion and collective punishment.
5. **Rule of law.** Any proposed institutions must be subject to public rules, independent review and meaningful remedies.
6. **Separation and accountability of powers.** No person, office, party, platform operator or technical system should exercise unchecked public power.
7. **Transparency.** Governance rules, funding sources where safely disclosable, decision records and project results should be open to scrutiny.
8. **Privacy and safety.** Collect only necessary data, protect sensitive information and assess foreseeable risks before deployment.
9. **Accessibility.** Participation should be designed for different languages, abilities, connectivity levels and levels of technical literacy.
10. **Evidence and correction.** Claims should be sourced, uncertainty disclosed and errors corrected openly.

## 3. Community governance

CEP intends to be governed by a pluralistic community under publicly documented rules. This is a founding commitment, but the community and its procedures must be built legitimately rather than declared into existence.

Until a ratified governance framework exists:
- this charter and related documents remain proposals;
- contributors may discuss, fork, criticise and propose amendments;
- repository maintainers may perform technical moderation and protect infrastructure, but must not present those actions as a public mandate;
- material governance changes should be announced with reasons and an opportunity for review; and
- the repository owner must disclose that GitHub administrative control remains technically centralised at this stage.

A future ratification process should define eligibility, representation, conflicts of interest, accessibility, quorum, voting or consensus methods, appeal rights, amendment thresholds and safeguards against capture. These details are open questions, not silently assumed rules.

## 4. Political and constitutional neutrality

CEP may host research and debate about competing systems—including parliamentary and presidential republics, constitutional monarchies, federal and decentralised arrangements, and other democratic models. It must not endorse one as the predetermined outcome or claim that a contributor speaks for a nation.

Country-specific missions must respect local context, diverse viewpoints, human rights and the right of affected people to determine their future through credible, free and inclusive processes.

## 5. Public-interest work and incentives

Any bounty or funding mechanism must reward verifiable public benefit, not political loyalty, recruitment, intimidation, propaganda, participation in a particular faction or support for a predetermined constitutional result. Political rights and governance influence must never be purchasable. No token balance should confer additional civic voting power.

## 6. Safety and non-harm

CEP will not intentionally build tools for surveillance, doxxing, coercion, political targeting or exposing vulnerable participants. Sensitive political participation data should not be collected unless a compelling, lawful and independently reviewed need is demonstrated with robust safeguards. Threat modelling and privacy review are prerequisites for high-risk deployments.

## 7. Amendment and ratification

This draft may be amended through public pull requests and discussion. Acceptance into the Git repository means only that a version is published; it does not by itself mean that a representative community has ratified it. A separate, transparent ratification process must be designed and documented before the charter is treated as binding community governance.

## 8. Invitation

Help challenge this charter. Identify omissions, propose safeguards, improve its accessibility and translate it. CEP should earn trust through its conduct, not ask for trust in advance.
