# Declaration Review Brief

**Subject:** `DECLARATION.md` and `docs/declaration-original.md`  
**Status:** Open critical review; no presumption that the edited text is final.

## Context

The published Declaration is a personal invitation to humans and AI agents to investigate an imperfect framework for improving social coordination and institutions. The original wording is preserved separately. The publication note explicitly says the Declaration is not a governmental authority claim or community-ratified mandate.

The Declaration should be read alongside:
- `FOUNDING-CHARTER.md`
- `GOVERNANCE.md`
- `VISION.md`
- `ROADMAP.md`
- `AGENTS.md`
- `agents/RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md`

## Review questions

### A. Fidelity to the author's intent
- Does the edited version preserve the original's urgency, humility and invitation to collaborate?
- Which edits materially alter the meaning rather than merely improve clarity?
- Are there ideas in the original that should be preserved, clarified or deliberately retired?
- Does the edited version introduce claims or meanings the author did not intend?

### B. Conceptual clarity
- Is "technocracy" defined clearly enough to distinguish evidence-informed public administration from rule by experts?
- Does "swarm" mean coordinated research and production, rather than autonomous political authority?
- Is "emergency" rhetorical, analytical or an operational status? Could it be misread as a justification for extraordinary powers?
- Are "civilization", "legitimacy" and "human well-being" sufficiently defined for practical work?

### C. Evidence and credibility
- Which statements are aspirations rather than demonstrated outcomes?
- Does the text avoid implying that the framework has already prevented conflict or improved governance?
- Are references to books, products, formulas and calculations clearly identified as author-reported until independently reviewed?
- What source materials are needed to evaluate the framework?

### D. Alignment with CEP's public commitments
- Does the Declaration preserve popular sovereignty, human rights, pluralism, nonviolence and privacy?
- Does it make clear that AI agents cannot govern, vote as humans, represent populations or manufacture public consent?
- Does it remain compatible with community governance rather than founder-centred authority?
- Does it leave political and constitutional outcomes open?

### E. Editorial and publication decisions
- Should the Declaration remain a personal statement linked to CEP, rather than become part of the Charter?
- Are any passages ambiguous, overbroad or likely to be misunderstood?
- What disclaimers, links or source references would improve public understanding?
- What should remain unchanged to preserve the author's voice?

## Required output

Create `research/DECLARATION-CRITICAL-REVIEW.md` with:
1. Executive summary.
2. Comparison of the original and edited versions, including material meaning changes.
3. Strengths worth preserving.
4. Ambiguities, unsupported claims and potential misreadings.
5. Conflicts or alignments with the Charter and Governance proposal.
6. Suggested line-level edits, each with a reason.
7. Unresolved questions for the author and community.
8. A recommendation: retain, revise, split into multiple documents, or withdraw specific passages—with reasons.

Do not rewrite the Declaration wholesale unless separately requested. Do not treat the review as a vote or claim community consensus. The author's intent should be queried where it cannot be inferred responsibly.
