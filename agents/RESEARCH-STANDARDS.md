# CEP Research and Evidence Standards

**Status:** Proposed standards for all human and AI research contributions.

## 1. Label the kind of statement

Use these distinctions consistently:

- **Fact:** supported by a reliable source or directly reproducible observation.
- **Author statement:** something the Declaration's author says or believes; not independently verified merely by publication.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation of what evidence or wording may mean.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposition that may be supported or falsified.
- **Proposal:** a recommended design or course of action.
- **Speculation:** a possibility for which evidence is currently insufficient.

Do not turn a hypothesis, proposal or author's statement into a fact through confident wording.

## 2. Source requirements

Prefer primary sources, official datasets, peer-reviewed research, reputable institutional reports and transparent technical documentation. Use secondary sources to provide context or critique.

For each important source, record:
- author or issuing institution;
- title;
- publication date and URL or persistent identifier;
- source type;
- the exact claim it supports;
- relevant limitations, funding or conflicts where known.

Do not fabricate citations, quotations, datasets, interviews or source access. If a source cannot be checked, label it as unverified.

## 3. Claims and evidence table

For major claims, use a table with:

| Claim | Type | Evidence | Counterevidence | Confidence | Next test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Confidence should be explained in words and tied to evidence quality—not presented as false numerical precision.

## 4. Causal claims

For claims that a policy or system reduces war, corruption, poverty or instability:
- define the outcome precisely;
- identify the proposed causal mechanism;
- distinguish correlation from causation;
- consider confounders and alternative explanations;
- specify baseline, comparison and time horizon;
- state what result would count against the claim.

Avoid claiming that CEP will produce a societal outcome before it has been tested.

## 5. Comparative institutional analysis

When comparing governance systems:
- define the models and variants being compared;
- use consistent criteria;
- include advantages, disadvantages and failure modes;
- consider context and implementation capacity;
- include minority rights and peaceful transfer of power;
- avoid ranking models without transparent criteria and evidence.

## 6. AI-specific integrity

AI agents must:
- disclose material AI assistance;
- identify model-generated inferences and uncertain facts;
- verify citations against the cited source;
- avoid citing search snippets as if they were full studies;
- report failed searches and unavailable documents;
- preserve dissenting interpretations;
- avoid generating artificial endorsements, personas or apparent consensus.

## 7. Privacy and safety

Do not collect or publish identifying details of people involved in sensitive civic or political activity. Use aggregate, public or synthetic data wherever possible. Do not infer sensitive traits or expose private communications. Flag any research that could enable surveillance, targeting, coercion or retaliation.

## 8. Reproducibility and review

Include search terms, date ranges, inclusion criteria, methods, code or calculations where relevant. Separate the evidence review from the recommendations. State what another researcher would need to reproduce the work.

A well-supported conclusion may be that a proposal is not viable, cannot currently be tested, or should not be built.
