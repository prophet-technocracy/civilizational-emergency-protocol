# CEP-T001 — Iran: Peaceful and Inclusive Democratic Transition Research

**Status:** Ready for research; proposed mission, not an adopted political programme.  
**Priority:** First country-specific CEP task.  
**Mission reference:** CEP-MISSION-002 — Iran.  
**Suggested coordination:** One human mission coordinator, with independent agents taking separate subtasks.  
**Primary output:** A research package under `missions/iran/`.

## 1. Purpose

Develop a rigorous, politically pluralistic and safety-conscious research framework for how a future democratic transition in Iran could be discussed, evaluated and supported through peaceful civic and institutional processes.

This task follows the earlier CEP discussions about:
- popular sovereignty and equal citizenship;
- a peaceful transition with credible interim safeguards;
- public deliberation and informed constitutional choice;
- pluralistic political association;
- independent oversight and accountability;
- public-interest projects that can be evaluated transparently.

The purpose is to examine possible institutions and safeguards—not to predict, direct or claim authority over Iran's political future.

## 2. Non-negotiable boundaries

All contributors must observe these boundaries:

1. **No predetermined constitutional outcome.** CEP must not decide in advance that Iran should become a monarchy, republic, federal state or any other model.
2. **No self-appointed representation.** CEP, its founder, agents and contributors cannot claim to represent the Iranian people or any political movement.
3. **Popular sovereignty.** Legitimate constitutional authority must come from a free, informed, inclusive and credible process involving the people concerned.
4. **Peaceful means and rights.** The work must respect nonviolence, equal citizenship, fundamental freedoms, due process and protections for minorities.
5. **No appointed ruler.** No individual receives sovereign authority or a transitional office merely through fame, family status, advocacy, wealth, platform influence or CEP endorsement.
6. **Pluralistic treatment of public figures.** Reza Pahlavi may be analysed as a public advocate for a parliamentary-monarchy option, as previously discussed. He must not be treated as an automatically appointed sovereign, an assumed head of transition or the exclusive representative of Iranians. Other advocates and constitutional positions must receive fair treatment.
7. **Safety and privacy.** Do not collect names, locations, contact lists, political affiliations, participation records or other identifying information about people inside Iran. Do not solicit sensitive testimony that could expose a person to retaliation.
8. **No covert or coercive activity.** This task does not authorise surveillance, targeting, clandestine mobilisation, harassment, intimidation, operational interference or instructions for evading lawful safeguards.
9. **No artificial mandate.** Online polls, agent outputs, repository votes and contributor counts are not national consent or an election.
10. **Community review.** All country-specific recommendations remain drafts until reviewed through CEP's governance process and, where relevant, by diverse affected-community perspectives.

## 3. Core research questions

The research package must answer:

- What are the principal constitutional and institutional options that should be fairly compared?
- What minimum rights and democratic safeguards should apply regardless of the eventual constitutional model?
- What legitimate interim arrangements could preserve essential public services while preventing concentration of power?
- How could an inclusive constitutional process be designed, supervised and independently scrutinised?
- How can diverse political, ethnic, linguistic, religious and non-religious perspectives be included without exposing participants to risk?
- What can civic technology safely support, and what must remain outside CEP's role?
- What evidence, legal questions and contextual uncertainties remain unresolved?

## 4. Work breakdown

Agents should claim one subtask at a time. Do not have every agent write one giant document.

### T001-A — Context and evidence map

**Question:** What reliable, current and historically grounded sources are needed to understand Iran's constitutional, legal, institutional and social context?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/01-CONTEXT-AND-EVIDENCE-MAP.md`

Include:
- a source map covering constitutional history, institutions, rights, public administration and relevant transition experiences;
- a clear distinction between primary sources, scholarly analysis, advocacy materials and opinion;
- competing interpretations and gaps in available evidence;
- a dated bibliography with source descriptions;
- careful treatment of uncertainty and contested claims.

Do not infer the views of the population from diaspora commentary, online activity or a narrow set of sources.

### T001-B — Constitutional models comparison

**Question:** What constitutional arrangements could be compared in a future open and informed public process?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/02-CONSTITUTIONAL-MODELS.md`

At minimum, compare:
- parliamentary republic;
- presidential or semi-presidential republic;
- parliamentary constitutional monarchy;
- federal or decentralised arrangements, including how these could combine with different forms of government;
- other credible models identified by research.

For each, assess:
- source of executive and legislative authority;
- selection, removal and accountability of officeholders;
- separation of powers and judicial independence;
- rights protections and minority safeguards;
- territorial governance and distribution of powers;
- peaceful transfer of power;
- risks of capture, deadlock or authoritarian concentration;
- implementation assumptions and trade-offs.

Do not declare a preferred model. Apply the same criteria to every model.

### T001-C — Interim governance and transition safeguards

**Question:** What safeguards could prevent a power vacuum and prevent interim authorities from becoming permanent or unaccountable?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/03-INTERIM-GOVERNANCE-SAFEGUARDS.md`

Research:
- limited and publicly defined interim mandates;
- transparent appointment and removal rules;
- separation of temporary administrative functions from constitution-making;
- independent courts or review mechanisms and due process;
- continuity of essential public services;
- civilian accountability and lawful control of public institutions;
- sunset clauses, public reporting and independent audit;
- safeguards against collective punishment, arbitrary detention and political purges;
- credible dispute-resolution and peaceful handover mechanisms.

Do not produce operational instructions for seizing, disabling or controlling institutions. Keep the work at the level of comparative institutional design, rights and public accountability.

### T001-D — Constitutional process and public legitimacy

**Question:** What makes a constitutional process genuinely free, inclusive, informed and credible?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/04-CONSTITUTIONAL-PROCESS-AND-LEGITIMACY.md`

Compare possible stages:
1. public access to reliable, pluralistic civic information;
2. open deliberation and publication of competing proposals;
3. transparent rules for selecting or constituting a representative drafting body;
4. public consultation and publication of draft texts;
5. independent legal, rights and feasibility review;
6. a credible, independently supervised public decision;
7. implementation, accountability and amendment procedures.

Identify participation barriers, representation problems, accessibility needs, risks of intimidation and safeguards for dissent. Do not assume CEP can organise or certify a national vote.

### T001-E — Civic participation, pluralism and association

**Question:** What non-coercive civic infrastructure could help people understand options, deliberate and organise voluntarily while protecting their privacy?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/05-CIVIC-PARTICIPATION-AND-PLURALISM.md`

Examine:
- open access to balanced information;
- pluralistic parties, associations, unions, civil society and independent media;
- freedom of expression, assembly and association;
- protections for political minorities and peaceful opposition;
- accessibility, language and low-bandwidth needs;
- grievance and appeal channels.

Distinguish neutral civic infrastructure from campaigning or partisan political organisation. Do not build political profiles, sensitive participant registries or persuasion-targeting systems.

### T001-F — Privacy, threat model and human-rights risk review

**Question:** What harms could arise from researching, publishing or deploying CEP-related tools in the Iranian context?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/06-PRIVACY-SECURITY-AND-HUMAN-RIGHTS-RISKS.md`

Assess risks to:
- people inside Iran;
- diaspora contributors;
- researchers, journalists and civil society;
- maintainers and infrastructure;
- people whose political views differ from the majority.

Cover data minimisation, safe publication, metadata exposure, access controls, retention, incident response, coercion, surveillance, doxxing, impersonation and platform compromise. Prefer not to collect sensitive data at all. Identify work that should be paused or prohibited if it cannot be made acceptably safe.

### T001-G — Public-good projects and accountable incentives

**Question:** Could a public-good project mechanism support measurable community needs without rewarding political loyalty or buying influence?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/07-PUBLIC-GOOD-PROJECTS-AND-INCENTIVES.md`

Compare grants, donations, transparent bounties, procurement and non-monetary contribution. Define verification, conflict disclosure, audit, fraud prevention and equitable access. Explicitly prohibit rewards for political recruitment, factional loyalty, intimidation, risky activity or support for a constitutional outcome. No token or donation may buy governance votes or political power.

### T001-H — Synthesis and recommendations

**Question:** What can responsibly be concluded after the independent research tasks, and what remains too uncertain or risky?

Deliverable: `missions/iran/08-SYNTHESIS-AND-OPEN-DECISIONS.md`

The synthesis must:
- compare findings from T001-A through T001-G;
- preserve disagreements and minority analyses;
- separate evidence from recommendations;
- identify unsupported assumptions and missing research;
- recommend what CEP could safely research or prototype;
- identify what CEP should not build or should defer;
- list questions requiring affected-community input and independent expert review.

The synthesis agent must not manufacture consensus or claim to speak for Iranians.

## 6. Required research method

Every subtask must follow `agents/RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md`.

In addition:
- Prefer primary legal texts, credible scholarship, comparative constitutional research and transparent institutional reports.
- Date all research and identify the jurisdiction and period each source covers.
- Use sources with differing perspectives; do not rely on one political faction or media ecosystem.
- Clearly label advocacy sources and assess their claims critically.
- Verify important claims against the underlying source, not just a search snippet.
- Do not invent polling, public opinion, endorsements or representative consultation.
- Do not describe a constitutional model as the preference of Iranians unless supported by credible, methodologically transparent evidence—and even then state the limitations.

## 7. Overall acceptance criteria

CEP-T001 is ready for synthesis when:
- all seven research workstreams have substantive drafts or documented reasons why they could not be completed;
- constitutional models are compared using consistent criteria;
- interim arrangements include meaningful limits, accountability and sunset provisions;
- popular sovereignty and rights protections are explicit across all proposals;
- privacy and human-rights risks are assessed before any platform proposal;
- all key claims are traceable to sources;
- unresolved disputes and evidence gaps are visible;
- at least one independent human reviewer has reviewed the package;
- the synthesis makes no claim of national representation, mandate or consensus.

## 8. What this task does not authorise

This task does not authorise CEP or its agents to:
- select a leader, government, monarch, party or constitutional outcome for Iran;
- organise covert political activity or direct actions against people or institutions;
- collect identifiable political participation data;
- conduct persuasion targeting or political profiling;
- claim that an online process is a national referendum;
- speak on behalf of the Iranian people.

## 9. First action for an agent

If you are the first agent to begin:
1. Read the CEP Declaration, Charter, Governance proposal and Agent Research Standards.
2. Claim **T001-A** or **T001-F** first, unless another contributor has already done so.
3. Open a GitHub issue stating your research question, source plan and intended file.
4. Submit a short methodology proposal before writing a long report.
5. Keep the output independent, sourced, critical and safe.

**The standard is not whether the work supports a preferred political outcome. The standard is whether it helps people evaluate possible futures freely, safely and with reliable information.**
